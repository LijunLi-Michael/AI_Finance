# AI Finance Optional Further Reading

*Historical technical and critical context for the course*

# **How to use this list**

None of this is required, graded, or included on a quiz. The list is for students who want to understand where the ideas in AI Finance came from, how they developed, and what happens when financial and technical assumptions fail.

Everything below is free and opens in a browser. Nothing needs to be purchased or downloaded.

# **If you only read three things**

* **★ Nick Szabo  Trusted Third Parties Are Security Holes  2 pages**  
* **★ Satoshi Nakamoto  Bitcoin A Peer to Peer Electronic Cash System  9 pages**  
* **★ Briola Vidal Tomás Wang and Aste  Anatomy of a Stablecoins Failure  short paper**

The problem, the proposed answer, and what happens when the design fails. In that order, the three readings take about an hour.

# **1 Where money comes from**

## [**★ Nick Szabo — Trusted Third Parties Are Security Holes (2001)**](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/trusted-third-parties/)

Two pages. The core problem, stated clearly seven years before Bitcoin existed. Read this first; everything else in the list makes more sense afterward.

## [**Nick Szabo — Shelling Out The Origins of Money (2002)**](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/shelling-out/)

A long essay on why people used collectibles as money tens of thousands of years before banks. Reading the first few sections is enough to understand the central idea.

## [**F A Hayek — Choice in Currency A Way to Stop Inflation (1976)**](https://www.iea.org.uk/sites/default/files/publications/files/upldbook409.pdf)

Read Hayek's essay at the beginning; the later commentary is optional. The value is not in deciding whether he was right, but in seeing that government issuance of money is an assumption that can be questioned.

# **2 The attempts that failed and the one that did not**

## [**Tim May — The Crypto Anarchist Manifesto (1988)**](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/crypto-anarchist-manifesto/)

A brief statement of the culture and political imagination from which Bitcoin later emerged.

## [**Eric Hughes — A Cypherpunks Manifesto (1993)**](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/cypherpunk-manifesto/)

Read this together with Tim May's manifesto for the tone as much as the argument.

Three predecessors to skim in fifteen minutes:

* [Adam Back — Hashcash 1997](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/hashcash/)  
* [Wei Dai — b money 1998](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/b-money/)  
* [Nick Szabo — Bit Gold 2005](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/bit-gold/)

All three are cited in the Bitcoin whitepaper. Each gets much of the way toward digital money but stalls on a crucial problem. Finding that point is a fast way to see what was new in 2008.

## [**★ Satoshi Nakamoto — Bitcoin A Peer to Peer Electronic Cash System (2008)**](https://bitcoin.org/bitcoin.pdf)

Nine pages. If time is short, read Sections 1–5 for the design and Section 11 for the probability that an attacker catches up to the honest chain. Most readers need more than one pass.

## [**Hal Finney — Bitcoin and Me (2013)**](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/bitcoin-and-me/)

A five-minute personal account by the first person other than Satoshi to run the software. Read this rather than a price chart if you want to understand why people care about the field.

# **3 Programmable money and how it breaks**

## [**Vitalik Buterin — A Prehistory of the Ethereum Protocol (2017)**](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2017/09/14/prehistory.html)

Ethereum's early history from its designer, including ideas that were removed and why.

## [**Analysis of the DAO Exploit (2016)**](https://hackingdistributed.com/2016/06/18/analysis-of-the-dao-exploit/)

A technical account of the exploit that forced the Ethereum community to confront what code is law means in practice. Read it with the Ethereum history timeline below.

[Ethereum history timeline](https://ethereum.org/en/history/)

## [**★ Briola Vidal Tomás Wang and Aste — Anatomy of a Stablecoins Failure The Terra Luna Case (2022)**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.13914)

A concise academic post-mortem of the May 2022 collapse. For finance-oriented students, this is a case study in reflexivity and a death spiral expressed through tokens.

## [**Ian Allison — Divisions in Sam Bankman Frieds Crypto Empire Blur on His Trading Titan Alamedas Balance Sheet (2022)**](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2022/11/02/divisions-in-sam-bankman-frieds-crypto-empire-blur-on-his-trading-titan-alamedas-balance-sheet)

The balance-sheet reporting that preceded the collapse of FTX. Notice the analytical move: much of the reported asset base consisted of a token issued by the same corporate network.

## [**Nassim Nicholas Taleb — Bitcoin Currencies and Fragility (2021)**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.14204)

A short argument that Bitcoin is worth zero, written by a former supporter who changed his mind. Read it to understand a strong opposing case rather than only arguments that confirm your prior view.

# **One film**

## [**Cryptopia Bitcoin Blockchains and the Future of the Internet (2020)**](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Y2qe3hFeQ5g)

Directed by Torsten Hoffmann. 86 minutes. The film interviews believers and critics without resolving the dispute for the viewer.

[Film website and alternate access information](https://cryptopiafilm.com/)

Documentaries claiming to identify Satoshi rely on disputed circumstantial evidence. A more useful question is: if Satoshi's identity were confirmed tomorrow, what about Bitcoin would change, and what would remain the same?
